# BTCSunrise: Trading Jargon Primer

This guide breaks down the core structural variables used in our daily technical updates to help you translate raw market metrics into actionable distribution context.

---

## Macro Concepts

### Risk-Off vs. Risk-On Transitions
* **Risk-Off:** A market regime where global macro participants prioritize capital preservation over return optimization. Driven by unexpected geopolitical friction, hawkish monetary shifts, or system liquidity shocks, capital rapidly flees high-beta assets (like Bitcoin and altcoins) and seeks safe havens (U.S. Dollar, Treasuries, Gold).
* **Risk-On:** A regime where systemic conditions are stable or expansionary, encouraging capital deployment into aggressive asset classes to capture alpha.

## Understanding the Derivative Variables

### Open Interest (OI)
The aggregate value of all outstanding, un-liquidated derivative contracts (longs and shorts) active across major exchanges (Binance, OKX, Bybit). 
* **Utility:** High or expanding OI signals massive speculative fuel entering the system. When OI reaches extreme thresholds relative to spot depth, the market becomes highly sensitive to localized stop-hunts and volatility cascades.

### Predicted Funding Rate
Perpetual swaps do not have an expiration date. To keep the derivative contract price anchored to the true underlying index spot price, long or short positioning must exchange a structural premium fee every 8 hours.
* **Positive Funding Rates (+):** Longs are paying shorts. This demonstrates aggressive, leveraged bullish bias. Extreme positive spikes signal a top-heavy market susceptible to long squeezes.
* **Negative Funding Rates (-):** Shorts are paying longs. This signals overwhelming bearish positioning, leaving the market ripe for a violent short squeeze if a surprise bid arrives.

### Perpetual Contracts (Perps)
A type of derivative contract unique to crypto assets that functions similarly to a traditional futures contract but features **no expiry or settlement date**. Traders can maintain leverage indefinitely, provided they maintain sufficient margin requirements against real-time liquidation thresholds.

### 24h Liquidations (Long / Short)
The absolute dollar value of leveraged positions forcibly closed out by exchange engines due to a contract running out of required maintenance margin. 
* **Utility:** This tracks the body count. Heavy liquidations mean forced market orders are being slammed into the order book, creating deep technical wick extremes that often mark immediate, localized trend reversals.
