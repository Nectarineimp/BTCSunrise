## BTCSunrise Comments

### Holy Hack A-thon! How to keep your BTC safe

When high-profile centralized platforms and hot-wallet infrastructures suffer credential compromises or drain events, cybersecurity and cryptocurrency custody specialists consistently emphasize three operational principles:

1. **Strictly Limit Exchange Balances to Active Capital:** Treat any centralized exchange solely as an execution venue rather than a depository. Maintain only the liquidity necessary for immediate trading, limit orders, or rebalancing, and withdraw settled funds immediately.
2. **Treat Proof of Reserves (PoR) as an Accounting Snapshot, Not a Solvency Guarantee:** PoR demonstrates on-chain assets at a specific point in time, but does not account for off-chain liabilities, commingling, or sudden hot-wallet drainage. Users are advised to size exposure strictly according to what loss the venue could realistically absorb.
3. **Harden Account Access at the Protocol Level:**
* Replace SMS and app-based OTP authenticators with physical FIDO2/WebAuthn hardware security keys (e.g., YubiKeys) to eliminate SIM-swapping and reverse-proxy phishing.
* Mandate **withdrawal address whitelisting** with 48- to 72-hour security time-locks and enable global settings locks where available (preventing unauthorized address insertions even if session cookies or API keys leak).
* Disable or aggressively scope API keys with IP whitelists and disable withdrawal privileges on API tokens entirely.

### Are There Any "Safe" Exchanges?

In custody, **no exchange is entirely risk-free**. Crypto assets held on centralized exchanges are not backed by FDIC insurance (which only protects fiat cash deposits in eligible partner accounts, not digital assets).

However, exchanges exist on a spectrum of institutional maturity, cold-storage discipline, and regulatory oversight:

* **Tier-1 Regulated Custodians (Lowest Relative Risk):** Platforms like **Coinbase**, **Kraken**, and **Gemini** maintain long-standing security postures with extensive institutional cold storage (often reported at 95–98%+ offline), third-party financial audits, and isolated access controls. Kraken, for instance, has operated since 2011 without a core exchange wallet breach.
* **Operational Reality:** Even on the most secure exchanges, counterparty risk, regulatory freezes, legal custody disputes during bankruptcy, and insider credential compromises remain real vectors. The security consensus remains: *an exchange is safe for settlement, but unsuitable for long-term reserves*.
