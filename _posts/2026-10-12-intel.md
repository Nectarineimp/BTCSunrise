## BTCSunrise Comments

Howdy Risers, we have quite an interesting week ahead of us. One thing to note from todays report is this line:

Options open interest reached **$37.36 Billion** on Monday morning, with call options dominating at **60.5%** of total positioning. Deribit order books reflect heavy open interest clustering in out-of-the-money calls targeting the **$85,000**, **$90,000**, and **$100,000** strike prices for late Q4 expirations.

Speculators and institutions buy calls to bet on price gains, but dealers don't take directional bets—they hedge dynamically to remain delta-neutral. How they hedge is dictated entirely by delta and gamma. Delta measures how much an option's price changes for every $1 change in Bitcoin's spot price. It also represents:
- The probability of the option expiring in-the-money (ITM).
- The equivalent spot Bitcoin position represented by that contract.
If buyers open thousands of call contracts across $85k, $90k, and $100k strikes, dealers end up short thousands of calls.

Because a call option has positive delta, the dealer who sold it is short delta (they lose money if Bitcoin climbs). To neutralize that risk, the dealer must buy spot Bitcoin or perpetual futures.

Heavy aggregate delta exposure means dealers have massive, active hedging footprints directly tied to the current spot price.

This positioning reflects a classic bullish asymmetry in medium-to-long term positioning paired with heavy gamma and delta exposure heading into late Q4. It's time to rethink shorting Bitcoin, and start preparing for when the $90K breakout comes.
