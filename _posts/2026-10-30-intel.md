### Why did my portfolio take a huge hit?

Equity market weakness is a primary catalyst, but the disappearance of an institutional bid floor is primarily driven by how multi-asset risk mandates and cross-margin mechanics interact with market maker inventory.

When an apparent institutional floor vanishes, several structural dynamics explain why resting bids fail to materialize:
1. Cross-Asset VaR Shock and Gross De-Grossing
Institutional participants holding digital assets alongside tech equities and credit do not evaluate Bitcoin in isolation.
 * Value-at-Risk (VaR) breaches: When broader equity indices drop—particularly led by mega-cap tech or earnings misses—aggregate portfolio volatility spikes. Multi-strategy and systematic funds are forced by internal risk models to reduce overall gross leverage across all liquid books.
 * The "ATM Effect": Bitcoin trades 24/7 with deep spot and derivatives liquidity. When traditional markets sell off or margin calls hit equity and options books, liquid, unencumbered assets like BTC are sold first to raise cash, cover margin, or satisfy redemption pressures.
2. Market Maker Inventory Hedging and Spread Widening
What retail often interprets as an "institutional floor" is usually non-directional market maker (MM) depth rather than programmatic institutional accumulation.
 * Skewed inventory: When directional selling pressure intensifies, market makers rapidly accumulate long inventory on their bid side.
 * Pulling liquidity: To protect against adverse selection and inventory holding risk during elevated volatility, automated market makers widen bid-ask spreads and pull bids from the order book, stepping down depth several hundred (or thousand) dollars lower. The resting "floor" evaporates instantly because it was never conviction spot buying—it was delta-neutral quoting that stepped aside.
3. Basis and Cash-and-Carry Unwinding
A significant portion of institutional capital in Bitcoin is delta-neutral rather than long directional spot:
 * Many institutional desks run basis trades (long spot ETF / short CME futures) to harvest annualized yields.
 * When the futures basis compresses during a broader market drawdown, the hurdle rate for carrying the trade disappears relative to short-term Treasury yields or cash equivalents.
 * Desks unwind the trade by selling the spot leg and buying back the futures, which actively removes spot buy-side absorption and creates net selling pressure on spot venues.
4. Asymmetric Passive Execution
True institutional long accumulators (family offices, macro funds, corporate balance sheets) virtually never deploy market orders into an ongoing cascade:
 * Large desks use Time-Weighted or Volume-Weighted Average Price (TWAP/VWAP) algorithms configured with strict price collars, or they rely on OTC principal liquidity desks.
 * If derivative liquidations (e.g., perpetual long cascades) are actively hitting the order books, institutional execution algorithms pause by design. Their mandate is to minimize slippage and adverse fill prices, so they will not post hard limit bids until liquidation open-interest resets and implied volatility surfaces stabilize.
The lack of a floor does not necessarily indicate a fundamental institutional exit; rather, institutional mandate constraints (VaR caps, cross-market margin defense) temporarily neutralize their ability to absorb risk, while programmatic algorithms intentionally step out of the way until forced liquidations finish clearing the books.
