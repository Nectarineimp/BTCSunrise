## BTCSunrise Comments

Hello early risers!

If you have been following this newsletter you can already predict what I am going to write. Why, if Bitcoin is such a good hedge against inflation, why does the price drop on bad inflation news in the United States?

Bitcoin drops on hot U.S. inflation news because **short-term market pricing is driven by Federal Reserve liquidity expectations rather than long-term monetary debasement**.

While Bitcoin’s hard cap of 21 million coins makes it a strong hedge against multi-year fiat debasement and M2 money supply expansion, four immediate mechanisms override that thesis on CPI release days:

1. **The Rate-Hike Reaction Function:** Hot inflation data (such as a hotter-than-expected CPI print) signals that the Federal Reserve will keep interest rates higher for longer or tighten policy further. Because Bitcoin trades as a high-beta, liquidity-sensitive asset in the short term, expectations of tighter monetary policy immediately compress valuations.
2. **Rising Real Yields & Opportunity Cost:** Higher inflation expectations push U.S. Treasury yields up. Because Bitcoin pays no yield or dividend, rising risk-free yields increase the opportunity cost of holding it, prompting capital to rotate into cash and short-term bonds.
3. **US Dollar (DXY) Strength:** Hawkish Fed expectations widen interest-rate differentials in favor of the U.S. dollar. A surging dollar tightens global financial conditions and creates mechanical downward pressure on dollar-denominated assets—including both Bitcoin and gold.
4. **Institutional Portfolio Risk-Off & Leverage:** With institutional desks, ETFs, and derivatives dominating daily volume, macro algorithms treat a hot CPI print as a broad "risk-off" signal. Leveraged long positions are liquidated first, amplifying the initial drop before spot buyers step back in.

**The Timeframe Split:** Bitcoin hedges against **currency debasement** (central banks printing money to cover deficits over years), not **price inflation** (consumer prices rising while the central bank actively drains liquidity to fight it).
